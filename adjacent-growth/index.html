Adjacent market development

Adjacent growth, accelerated and sustained.

Most companies do adjacent-market work properly once, and then never again. Not for lack of budget and not for lack of intent — but because doing it properly demands four capabilities at once, and the people who could lead it are already fully committed.

The wall

Adjacent growth is hard. Embedded adjacent growth is nearly impossible.

Done properly it demands four different capabilities, at the same time, every time.

Information consumptionEnormous volume, read in full

Not skimmed and not sampled. The document that decides the question is usually not the one that looked promising.

Skilled analysisOnly by highly skilled people

Significant analysis, by people with the technical training to know what a result actually means.

Tremendous memoryTotal recall, with reasons

What was found, and why it was judged the way it was — recoverable a year later, not remembered.

Clear recommendationOr none of it counts

Communicated concisely and effectively. Work that cannot be acted on is work that was not done.

Why does adjacent-market work get done once and then stop?
Because each of those four capabilities costs money, man-hours and experience, and in most organisations they are not one person but an entire group assembled across functions. A group that expensive can be held together for one effort. It cannot be held together permanently, so the effort is not repeated.
The volume wall. This is the constraint PROWL was built against.
The people

And who would actually do it?

The work needs four things at once. Each is common on its own. Together they are rare.

Deep chemistry

Reads a patent and knows what the molecule really does.

Commercial judgement

Knows which application is worth a call, and which is noise.

Weeks or months

And the mandate to spend them reading rather than selling.

Distillation and positioning

Compresses it into a case management will actually back.

All four, in one person. Most companies have one or two people capable of this. So in practice it is not a person at all — it is a complex team, assembled across functions and held together for months. And the people who could lead it are already fully committed. That is the constraint. Not budget, and not intent.

What changes

From a programme somebody has to champion, to a cadence the team just runs.

TodayA programme

Adjacent-market work runs when there is a budget, a sponsor and somebody willing to champion it.

It gets done properly once — and then not again. That is the wall.

EmbeddedA cadence

Every chemistry starts where the last one stopped. The second product is easier to analyse than the first, and the sixth easier still.

New business stops being an initiative. Nobody has to champion it.

Three things are kept, not rebuilt each time:

The governed vocabulary

Your ontology — the map of applications in your company's own words. At revision 19 on the engagement it came from.

The served baseline

What you already sell, corrected by you, so it is never handed back to you as a discovery.

The insight store

Where superseded findings stay pointered rather than deleted, so you can see what replaced what, and when.

What compounds, and what does not?
What compounds is the evidence and the vocabulary — not the judgement. Every gate in the process still needs a person to decide it, and the last one still needs a signature. The engine gets easier to run and sharper each cycle. It does not get more able to choose, and it is not meant to.
Stated plainly because it is the difference between a tool that helps and a claim that cannot be kept.
