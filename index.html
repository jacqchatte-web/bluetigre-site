What is in the way

Building adjacent sales growth is the hard part.

Not the reading — the reading is only where it starts. Turning a molecule into revenue in an industry you have never sold to has to get past three separate walls, and only one of them is about budget.

Wall 1Finding it

The evidence is public but scattered, and most of it is hard to read on purpose: dossiers written by lawyers for regulators, patents drafted to disclose as little as the law allows, papers written for specialists in another field. The decisive line sits in a clause.

Wall 2Proving it is worth selling

That needs someone who reads a patent and knows what the molecule really does, and knows which application is worth a call, and can build the case management will back. Most companies have one or two such people. They are already committed.

Wall 3Doing it again next quarter

Done properly the work is a team assembled across functions and held together for months. That is affordable once. So adjacent growth gets done properly once — and then not again.

And no, search does not solve it.

Why not a search engineIt ranks popularity, not evidence

Search returns what is linked to, not what is load-bearing. The document that decides whether your molecule can serve an application is often cited by nobody, written for a different discipline, and sitting on page nine.

Why not a general AIIt answers from memory, not from the record

A general-purpose model answers from what it absorbed in training — a summary of the internet at some past date, not a reading of the primary record. It will give you a fluent answer with no source you can check, and it cannot tell you what it failed to find.

Why not your own filesThe inside view is stale, and already explored

The material inside the company is dated, and the same ground has been walked repeatedly. The places that are genuinely new are, by definition, the ones nobody in the building has looked at.

What changes it

It is a new workflow, not a search.

A search answers a question once and leaves the business unchanged. A workflow runs on a cadence and is fitted to your company — your vocabulary, what you already sell, your own systems wired in alongside the public record. That is what decides whether the returns are a one-off.

Tailored AI is what makes it possible. A workflow like this was never really an option before, because the reading alone consumed the budget — so the work could only be afforded once. An engine fitted to your vocabulary and your evidence does that reading at a cost that makes repeating it sensible, and it remembers why every judgement was made, which is what lets a cadence run at all. It decides nothing. A person still signs.
