Three questions usually decide whether an adjacent market is worth entering: how big is the slice you would actually sell into, can you charge a premium, and who do you sell it through. 2 · What is in the way

Not budget, and not intent. Skill, time, and material that resists being read.

The question — where else could this sell? — is easy to ask and expensive to answer. Three things block it, and only one of them is about money.

The peopleFour rare things, in one head

It takes someone who reads a patent and knows what the molecule really does, and knows which application is worth a call, and has months to read rather than sell, and can compress it into a case management will back. Most companies have one or two such people. They are already committed.

The volumeAn entire group, not a person

Done properly the work needs enormous reading, skilled analysis, total recall of why each judgement was made, and a clear recommendation. In most organisations that is a team assembled across functions — affordable once, not permanently.

The materialHard to read on purpose

Dossiers written by lawyers for regulators. Patents drafted to disclose as little as the law allows. Academic papers written for specialists in someone else's field. The decisive line is in a clause or a supplementary table.

And no, search does not solve it.

Why not a search engineIt ranks popularity, not evidence

Search returns what is linked to, not what is load-bearing. The document that decides whether your molecule can serve an application is often cited by nobody, written for a different discipline, and sitting on page nine.

Why not a general AIIt answers from memory, not from the record

A general-purpose model answers from what it absorbed in training — a summary of the internet at some past date, not a reading of the primary record. It will give you a fluent answer with no source you can check, and it cannot tell you what it failed to find.

Why not your own filesThe inside view is stale, and already explored

The material inside the company is dated, and the same ground has been walked repeatedly. The places that are genuinely new are, by definition, the ones nobody in the building has looked at.

3 · The translation

From a chemical, to what it does, to who needs that.

This is the step that is usually missing. Searching a name returns the markets you already serve, because those are the people who already use that word. The industries that need it next do not call it what you call it — so the work runs on function and performance, not on nomenclature.

FirstChemistry into performance

What the substance does — interfacial behaviour, thermal and oxidative stability, biological activity, compatibility — as measured and published, not as claimed.

ThenPerformance into industries

Spanning the globe. Every sector and every region that needs that behaviour, in whatever words each of them uses for it — and every near-identical name that would otherwise pollute the result.

ThenIndustries into your markets

Grouped through your own vocabulary, with what you already serve subtracted, so what remains is genuinely new to you.

4 · From markets to a strategy

A ranked list is not a plan. The next question is how you would actually sell it.

Every surviving candidate is taken through three questions — is it real, is it worth it, can we win — from named public sources. What comes out is not a score but the beginning of a commercial strategy: who buys, who is already there, the route in, and the demand driver behind it.

The sale, named

Does it sell as the molecule stands, or does it need formulation work first? Those are two different sales, to two different buyers, on two different timescales.

The route in

Direct to the formulator, or through specialty distribution. Who is incumbent, what they hold, and where the window is — measured in filings per year rather than in sentiment.

What is blocked, and why

Blocked markets are reported too, with what opening them would take. A closed door with a named lock is more useful than an open door that isn't there.

5 · Embedding it

It is a new workflow, not a search.

This is the part that decides whether the returns are a one-off. A search answers a question once and leaves the business unchanged. A workflow runs on a cadence, and it is fitted to your company — your vocabulary, your served baseline, and your own systems and data wired in alongside the public record.

Embedded, not championed

It runs whether or not anyone is currently sponsoring it. New business stops being an initiative that needs a sponsor to survive.

Every chemistry cheaper than the last

The vocabulary, the served baseline and the fitting carry forward. The second product is easier than the first, and the sixth easier still.

Evidence that does not go stale

Live leads are re-walked on a cadence and compared on the source text itself, so you hear from us when something genuinely moved — not when it was reworded.

7 · The long term

You end up owning the intelligence, and the layer that understands your business.

Most market work ends as a slide deck and everything learned making it is lost. This ends as an asset you keep: a sourced library of your markets, and an application layer tuned to how your company actually describes itself — which is what makes every cycle after the first sharper and cheaper.

The vocabulary, governed

Your ontology — your segment names, your words for what you already sell, your brands and divisions. Versioned, and every entry records where the wording came from.

The evidence, kept

Superseded findings stay pointered rather than deleted, so a year later you can see what replaced what, and when, and on whose say-so.

The fitting, done once

Your own systems and data connected, the engine adjusted to your workflow. Engineering work for your engagement — and the reason the second chemistry costs less than the first.

Is my data used to train an AI model?
No. Nothing in PROWL trains or fine-tunes a model on your data, and there is no such step in the system. What accumulates is the context the engine works from — your vocabulary, your served baseline, your exclusions and your evidence — held in your own files. Nothing you tell us is absorbed into model weights, which is why deleting your file genuinely deletes it.
What you keep is a library and a fitted configuration you own, not a share in somebody else’s model.
