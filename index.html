How PROWL works
PROWL · a new AI business-development engine

The AI does the reading. A person makes every judgement.

PROWL — Pattern Recognition Of White-space Leads — is a trained agentic AI that finds new markets for a chemical a company already makes or wants to evaluate, then tests whether each one is real, worth pursuing and winnable. Weeks of desk research become days. Every fact carries its source and date.

Stage 1Discover — find the opportunities

PROWL is given the chemical's identity and your current product lines. It reads eight kinds of public source — use databases, regulators, patents, scientific literature and the open web — and turns every documented use into a record with its evidence attached.

Uses are grouped into markets by a custom ontology built in your company's own business language. Markets you already serve are set aside. What remains is a ranked list of genuinely new opportunities.

Stage 2Qualify — build the business case

Each opportunity must answer three questions: Is it real? Is it worth it? Can we win? PROWL works through named public sources — free before paid — and writes every fact down with its source and date.

The AI drafts each conclusion; a person reviews and approves it before it goes anywhere. Approved conclusions feed a SWOT, a client-ready report and a pitch presentation, so every number you see traces back to a source.

Case study

81 candidate markets, 39 the client already served — and the top-ranked lead was wrong.

81

candidate markets found for one established chemical — a synthetic polyol ester base stock

39

markets the client already served, subtracted automatically before ranking

1

week from first run to a sourced go-to-market foundation, with the gaps named

The board's number-one lead was refrigeration systems. PROWL then read its own evidence back and rejected it: every document described the client's existing refrigeration-lubricant business seen from the equipment end. Not white space — the same business under a different name.

It did the same to the runner-up before promoting it. The heat-transfer evidence was thinner than the score implied — three documents, one applicant, and a sentence repeated verbatim across all three. The lead survived on what was left, not on the count.

What remained was a real one: transformer and dielectric fluids, qualified against three questions.

  • Is it real? Yes, for the chemistry — this exact family is already sold as a commercial dielectric fluid by a major, which proves the application rather than predicting it. Unproven for the client's own grade, and the report says so.
  • Is it worth it? The demand signal is register-verified, not brochure-claimed: official US volumes for both the molecule and its family more than doubled since 2020, while imports of the family's trade basket fell 41% — import substitution visible in two independent official sources. No fact yet sizes the transformer-fluid market itself, and that gap is stated rather than estimated.
  • Can we win? The regulatory file is clean on both sides of the Atlantic, with three new EU registrants since 2022 saying the family is growing. The incumbents, the patents and the bio-based label currently sit with others — so the window is measured in filings per year, and the report says what closing it takes.

A week to a sourced foundation — and, as importantly, two leads killed with reasons on the record. Every figure traces to its source, and nothing reached the client that a person had not approved.
