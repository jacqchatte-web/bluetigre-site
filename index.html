Spanning the globe

Your molecule does something. Somewhere in the world, an industry you have never sold to needs exactly that.

Finding it is not a search. A chemical's name only returns the people already buying it — the markets you serve today. The industries that need it next do not call it what you call it. So the work starts by translating the substance into its functional language, and then runs across every sector and every region at once.

FirstTranslate

What the substance does — interfacial behaviour, stability, biological activity — as measured and published, not as claimed.

ThenSpan

Every industry, every region. Adhesives to agriculture, energy to personal care, in whatever words each of them uses for the same function.

ThenTest

Is it real, is it worth it, can we win — answered from named public sources, with the open questions stated rather than filled in.

The problem

This is a reading job that search engines and chatbots cannot do.

The evidence is public, but it is scattered, and most of it is hard to read on purpose — dossiers written by lawyers for regulators, patents drafted to disclose as little as the law allows, academic papers written for specialists in someone else's field. Reading it properly takes weeks, and it takes someone who can read a patent and know what the molecule really does and knows which application is worth a call.

Why not a search engineIt ranks popularity, not evidence

Search returns what is linked to, not what is load-bearing. The document that decides whether your molecule can serve an application is often cited by nobody, written for a different discipline, and sitting on page nine.

Why not a general AIIt answers from memory, not from the record

A general-purpose model answers from what it absorbed in training — a summary of the internet at some past date, not a reading of the primary record. It will give you a fluent answer with no source you can check, and it cannot tell you what it failed to find.

Why not your own filesThe inside view is stale, and already explored

The material inside the company is dated, and the same ground has been walked repeatedly. The places that are genuinely new are, by definition, the ones nobody in the building has looked at.

Does PROWL ever get it wrong?
Yes, and the engagement is designed to catch it. In the first study the top-ranked lead was refrigeration systems. PROWL read its own evidence back and rejected it: every document described the client’s existing refrigeration-lubricant business, seen from the equipment end. Not white space — the same business under another name.
