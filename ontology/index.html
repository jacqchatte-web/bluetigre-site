The knowledge store

A market means what your sales team means by it.

Uses are grouped into markets through an ontology — a map of applications written in your company's own vocabulary, not a chemical database's categories. It is the quiet piece of the engine, and it is the reason the second chemistry is easier than the first.

Why it exists

A database's category is not your category.

A registry might file a use under "industrial lubricants". Your business might call the same thing three different things, sell two of them, and have no interest in the third. If the grouping does not match how you actually describe your business, two things go wrong at once.

Your own business comes back as a discovery

Markets you already serve have to be subtractable before anything is ranked. In one engagement 39 of 81 candidate markets were already served and were removed automatically. Without a vocabulary that matches yours, those 39 would have been handed back to you as findings.

The genuinely new gets buried

What remains after the subtraction is the point of the whole exercise. If the grouping is wrong, the real white space sits underneath a pile of things you already do, and nobody reads that far down.

What is a PROWL ontology?
A map of applications written in your company’s own vocabulary, used to group individual documented uses into markets. A market is defined the way your sales team defines it, rather than the way a chemical database happens to categorise it.
It is built during the engagement and corrected by you. It is yours.
How it is governed

Versioned, reviewed, and logged with whose decision it was.

An ontology that anyone can quietly edit is worth nothing, because you can never tell whether a result changed for a good reason. So every change is proposed, reviewed against the evidence, and logged with a date. On one engagement it stood at revision 19.

Proposed

A change is suggested, with the evidence that prompted it attached.

Reviewed

Checked against that evidence by a person, not applied because a machine suggested it.

Logged

Dated, and recorded with whose say-so it was. You can always see what moved and when.

What is written down

Four things, each carrying its source and the date it was read.

The company and the market

The company — what you make and already sell, so served markets are subtracted before ranking.

The market — every candidate market and the facts behind it, each with its basis, its unit and its source.

The technology and the uses

The technology — properties as measured and published, not as claimed.

The uses — what the substance actually does, including how it behaves biologically, because that is often what decides where it can sell.

Two libraries, not one. What we learn about a market is public knowledge and carries no client name — it makes every study after yours faster. What you tell us lives in your own file and never crosses into the public one. The two are joined by a market reference and by nothing else. Delete your file and everything you gave us goes with it.

Why it compounds

The second chemistry is easier than the first.

The vocabulary and the served baseline carry forward. Nothing has to be rebuilt to start the next product, which is what turns adjacent-market work from a programme into a cadence you can sustain.

What compounds, and what does not?
The evidence and the vocabulary compound. The judgement does not. Every gate still needs a person to decide it, and the last one still needs a signature — so the engine gets easier to run and sharper each cycle without ever becoming more able to choose.
A sourced library your company keeps. It does not depreciate.
